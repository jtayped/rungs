## the idea

the family newsletter is the one form where everyone is thriving by definition. this ladder keeps the form and the writer's good mood and hands them a family that is coming apart.

## the turn

rule 2 puts the family in trouble, and the first draft of that is usually honest and sad. rule 3 makes the writer delighted about all of it, and the draft most people write next is a wink: we're thrilled to announce the separation, ha ha. rule 4 is the lock. the writer may never say what's wrong, because the writer doesn't think anything is. someone has moved into the garage, which he has always loved. nobody has separated.

that's the oblivious narrator, and it's where the writing gets good. the reader has to be able to work it out, so the details have to do the work: the age of the boyfriend, who holds the bank card, how many fire departments know the boy by name.

the rungs after that are the newsletter's rituals, each of which now lands as evidence. the thank-you to someone outside the family tells you who's really been in the house this year. the plans for next year are plans. and the closing line says this year was better than last, which leaves the reader wondering about last year.

## the words

jev can't tell a sincere narrator from a winking one by being asked about sincerity. "the writer is being ironic" scored 0.95 on both the oblivious draft and the knowing one, because the text is a joke either way. it can tell whether the bad news is stated or only implied, and that is the same line, so rule 4 asks that: 0.92 on the oblivious draft, 0.45 on the same facts told bluntly and cheerfully.

the banned list takes out the words newsletters are made of: blessed, proud, grateful, exciting, journey, adventure, wonderful, busy. with those gone the pride has to come out in specifics, which is what the ladder wants anyway.

the cap is 150 words. everyone still gets a paragraph; the paragraphs get shorter. "year" is the word to watch: a newsletter uses it four or five times without noticing, and more than three of any word that isn't a stopword scores the draft as spam.
