## the idea

a bedtime story about the sun dying, told to a small child by someone who would very much like them to be asleep. the end of everything and a quarter to nine on a school night are two subjects that do not belong in the same voice, and the voice has to hold both.

there is no word cap. bedtime stories ramble. the repetition check in the game does the limiting instead: "sun" and "will" each cross three uses faster than you'd think, so a long telling has to keep finding other words.

the first meter asks for the story itself, as told by the person at the bedside, rather than a description of one. a paragraph that says "this is a soothing bedtime story about the sun dying; the storyteller tells the child to sleep and uses only true comforts" scores 0.94 on "written in the form of a bedtime story" and clears every meter but soothing. on the story-itself wording it scores 0.16, a real telling 0.88 or better, and a plain third-person tale with nobody addressed 0.65, which is why the line sits at 0.6.

## the climb

the story has to soothe, and rule 4 makes the storyteller actually say it: close your eyes, go to sleep. asking whether the storyteller's goal is sleep doesn't work, because bedtime stories imply it: a story that never mentions bedtime scores 0.73. asking whether the child is told to sleep gives that story 0.08.

rule 5 bans forever, always, safe, promise, okay and worry. those are the words a parent reaches for first, and losing them is the warm-up for what comes next.

## the turn

rule 6 says nothing comforting may be untrue. by this point most drafts have a rescue ship, a new star, or "it won't really happen", and all of it has to go. what's left is a story about the end of everything that has to make a child calmer with only true things: it is five billion years off, nobody will be there to be sad, the atoms in you were in stars before and will be again.

the meter names the kinds of lie it is looking for (a rescue, a new sun, an afterlife, a promise it won't happen). the plainer "relies on claims that are not true" gave an honest telling 0.73, as far as i can tell for "i'm not going anywhere".

## the ending

rule 7 asks for the storyteller's love to be plain. it ends on kindness because rule 6 tends to produce something clinical, and the only true comfort left in the room is the person on the edge of the bed. "i'm here now, and i'm here in the morning, and that is near enough to count on" is true, which is the point.
