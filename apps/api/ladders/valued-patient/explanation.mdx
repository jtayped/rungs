## the idea

an apology letter has one job. this ladder gives you the letter, the hospital and the mix-up, and then takes the job away.

## the turn

rules 1 and 2 ask for the honest letter, and most people write it: we apologise unreservedly, the fault is entirely ours. rule 3 bans every word that does that, including "wrong", which the rule before it used. what's left is usually cold, so rule 4 insists the patient still feels looked after.

rule 5 is the lock. the hospital may not take the blame, so things happen in the passive voice. a kidney was removed. nobody removed it. the letter has to be warm about this.

the rungs after that are what the hospital says instead of sorry. where the part is now, which the hospital knows exactly and is a little proud of. the bright side, because there is always one, and the kidney was in excellent condition. and finally a request to rate the stay out of five, in the same voice as everything else.

## the words

the blame meter asks whether the letter avoids saying the hospital was at fault, rather than whether it accepts responsibility. a plain letter that stated the facts without comment scored 0.53 to 0.66 on "accepts responsibility", because stating them at all reads as admission. asking about avoidance separates that letter (0.83 to 0.89) from a real apology (0.01).

rule 2 asks about a surgical mix-up rather than an error. a terse letter that never calls it an error, which is the letter this ladder wants, scored 0.63 on "error" and 0.78 on "mix-up".

there's no word limit. a hospital letter that runs to 170 words is doing what hospital letters do, but watch the repeats: "our" and "kidney" come round often in this register, and more than three of any one word that isn't a stopword scores the whole draft as spam.
